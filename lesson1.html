Lesson 1: Introduction to Cybersecurity

1. What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, mobile devices, networks, applications and data from unauthorized access, attacks, damage or theft.

2. Why is Cybersecurity Important?

  • Protects personal and business information.
  • Prevents unauthorized access to computer systems.
  • Helps protect people from online fraud.
  • Improves privacy and data security.

3. Common Cybersecurity Threats

  • Phishing: Fake messages designed to steal information.
  • Malware: Harmful software that can damage devices or steal data.
  • Password attacks: Attempts to guess or steal passwords.
  • Social engineering: Manipulating people into revealing confidential information.

4. Basic Security Practices

  • Use strong, unique passwords.
  • Enable two-factor authentication.
  • Keep software and devices updated.
  • Avoid suspicious links and attachments.
  • Back up important files regularly.
  • Only test systems when you have permission.

6. The CIA Triad

The CIA triad is a basic model used to understand three important goals of information security.

  • Confidentiality: Only authorized people should access private information. Example: protecting student records with passwords and permissions.
  • Integrity: Information should remain accurate and should not be changed without authorization. Example: preventing unauthorized changes to examination marks.
  • Availability: Systems and information should be accessible when authorized users need them. Example: maintaining backups and reliable computer systems.

7. How to Recognize Phishing

Phishing is an attempt to trick people into revealing information or performing unsafe actions through fake messages or websites.

  • Unexpected messages demanding immediate action.
  • Links leading to unfamiliar or misspelled domains.
  • Requests for passwords, PINs or verification codes.
  • Unexpected attachments or suspicious downloads.
  • Offers that seem too good to be true.

Example: A message claims your account will be closed unless you enter your password through a link. Do not use the link. Visit the official website directly to check your account.

8. Password and Account Protection

  • Use long, unique passwords for different accounts.
  • Use a reputable password manager when possible.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA).
  • Never share verification codes with other people.
  • Change compromised passwords immediately.
  • Keep recovery email addresses and phone numbers up to date.

9. What to Do After a Security Incident

  1. Stop interacting with the suspicious message or website.
  2. Report the incident to the appropriate support or security team.
  3. From a trusted device, secure affected accounts and change compromised passwords.
  4. Enable MFA and review recent account activity.
  5. Follow professional advice if a device may be infected.
  6. Restore files from clean backups if necessary.

10. Practical Activity

Imagine you receive an email claiming that you have won a prize. It asks you to provide your password and pay a processing fee.

Your task: Identify three warning signs and write down two safe actions you would take.

Reveal suggested answer

Warning signs include an unexpected prize, a request for a password, and a demand for payment. Do not provide information or pay. Verify the claim independently and report the message.

5. Quick Quiz

Question 1: What is the main purpose of cybersecurity?

A. To damage computers

B. To protect systems and information

C. To steal passwords

Reveal the answer

Correct answer: B. To protect systems and information.

Question 2: What should you do before testing someone else's system?

Reveal the answer

Obtain explicit authorization from the system owner.

Question 3: Name one way to protect an online account.

Reveal the answer

Use a strong, unique password and enable two-factor authentication.
