Cybersecurity Fundamentals

Learn how to protect yourself and your devices online.

1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define cybersecurity.
  • Identify common online threats.
  • Create and manage strong passwords.
  • Recognise suspicious emails and messages.
  • Explain how to protect personal information.
  • Describe safe internet browsing practices.

2. Meaning of Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, mobile phones, networks, applications and information from unauthorised access, theft, damage and other digital attacks.

Why is cybersecurity important?

  • It protects personal information.
  • It helps prevent online fraud and identity theft.
  • It protects school and business records.
  • It reduces the risk of viruses and malicious software.
  • It helps keep online accounts secure.

3. Common Cybersecurity Threats

A. Phishing

Phishing is an attempt to trick people into revealing passwords, financial details or other sensitive information through fake emails, websites, messages or phone calls.

Example: You receive a message claiming that your account will be closed unless you click a suspicious link and enter your password.

B. Malware

Malware is software designed to harm devices, steal information or perform unwanted actions. Viruses, spyware and ransomware are examples of malware.

C. Ransomware

Ransomware is malicious software that can lock or encrypt files and demand payment for their recovery.

D. Social Engineering

Social engineering involves manipulating people into sharing confidential information or performing unsafe actions.

E. Identity Theft

Identity theft occurs when someone uses another person's identifying information without permission, often for fraud.

Remember: A message that creates panic or pressures you to act immediately may be a scam. Verify it independently before responding.

4. Creating Strong Passwords

A password helps prevent unauthorised access to an account. Weak or reused passwords make accounts easier to compromise.

Good password practices

  • Use a long, unique password for each account.
  • Consider a passphrase made of several unrelated words.
  • Use a reputable password manager to store passwords.
  • Never share your password with other people.
  • Enable multifactor authentication whenever possible.
  • Never send passwords through suspicious links or messages.
Example: A long, unique passphrase is generally safer than a short password such as "123456" or your name and birth year.

5. Protecting Against Phishing

Before clicking a link or opening an attachment:

  1. Check who sent the message.
  2. Inspect the website address carefully.
  3. Be suspicious of unexpected attachments.
  4. Do not share passwords or verification codes.
  5. Contact the organisation through its official website or phone number.
  6. Report suspicious messages using the appropriate reporting tools.

Remember that sender names and logos can be copied. A message that looks professional is not automatically genuine.

6. Safe Internet Browsing

  • Keep your operating system, browser and applications updated.
  • Download software from trusted official sources.
  • Use screen locks and device encryption where available.
  • Avoid entering sensitive information on suspicious websites.
  • Back up important files regularly.
  • Use secure networks and be cautious on public Wi-Fi.
  • Review app permissions and privacy settings.
  • Log out of shared devices when finished.

7. Protecting Personal Information

Personal information includes your full name, home address, phone number, identification numbers, passwords and financial information.

To protect it:

  • Share only what is necessary.
  • Review your social media privacy settings.
  • Do not publish passwords or verification codes.
  • Ask permission before sharing other people's information.
  • Be careful when using public computers.

8. What to Do If You Suspect an Attack

  1. Stop interacting with the suspicious message or website.
  2. Use a trusted device to change any compromised passwords.
  3. Enable multifactor authentication if available.
  4. Sign out other sessions and revoke unfamiliar account access.
  5. Notify the relevant service provider or school IT administrator.
  6. Scan affected devices with trusted security software.
  7. Restore affected files from clean backups if necessary.

If financial information was compromised, contact your bank promptly using its official contact details.

9. Knowledge Assessment

Answer all five questions and check your score.

1. What is cybersecurity?

2. What is phishing?

3. Which password practice is safest?

4. What should you do with a suspicious link?

5. What is one benefit of regular backups?

10. Lesson Summary

  • Cybersecurity protects digital systems and information.
  • Phishing and malware are common online threats.
  • Strong, unique passwords improve account security.
  • Verify unexpected messages before acting on them.
  • Updates and backups help protect your devices and files.
  • Report suspicious activity and respond promptly to compromised accounts.
